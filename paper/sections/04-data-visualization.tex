\section{Data Visualization: Seeing Patterns That Are Not Obvious from Tables}

Numbers can be easier to understand when they are visualized. Tables
provide detailed statistical values, while graphs help us see patterns
directly.

For this study, the main visualization is a scatter plot showing the
relationship between the total gadget-use score as variable X and the
total score for learning motivation and social interaction as variable Y.

In the scatter plot, each point represents one respondent.

The horizontal axis represents Gadget Use (X), while the vertical axis
represents Learning Motivation and Social Interaction (Y).

If the points tend to move from the lower-left area toward the upper-right
area, this indicates a positive relationship. In other words, higher X
scores tend to be accompanied by higher Y scores.

Conversely, if the points are widely scattered without a clear pattern,
the linear relationship between the two variables may be weaker.

Visualization is therefore an important part of statistical analysis
because some patterns are easier to identify through graphs than by
looking only at numerical values in a table.
