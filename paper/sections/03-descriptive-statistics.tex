\section{Descriptive Statistics: Turning \resN{} Responses into a Summary}

After understanding the research variables, the next step is to use
descriptive statistics. Descriptive statistics are used to examine the
central tendency and distribution of the data before analyzing
relationships between variables.

In this study, the responses to each item were summed to produce a total
score for variables X and Y for each respondent.

Variable X consisted of \resNXItems{} items, so its theoretical total
score ranged from
\[
\resNXItems \times 1 = \resXRangeMin
\]
to
\[
\resNXItems \times 5 = \resXRangeMax
\]
Therefore, the theoretical range of variable X was
\resXRangeMin--\resXRangeMax.

Variable Y consisted of \resNYItems{} items, so its theoretical score
ranged from
\[
\resNYItems \times 1 = \resYRangeMin
\]
to
\[
\resNYItems \times 5 = \resYRangeMax
\]
Therefore, the theoretical range of variable Y was
\resYRangeMin--\resYRangeMax.

\subsection{Calculation 1 --- Theoretical Score Range}

\[
X = \resXRangeMin\text{--}\resXRangeMax \qquad Y = \resYRangeMin\text{--}\resYRangeMax
\]

This calculation helps us understand the minimum and maximum scores that
could theoretically be obtained by a respondent.

Mean represents the average score of the respondents. Median represents
the middle value after all observations are arranged in order, while
standard deviation indicates how much the data are spread around the mean.

For example, the mean of X is \resXMean, the average gadget-use score
among the \resN{} students; similarly, the mean of Y is \resYMean, the
average score for learning motivation and social interaction.

A Shapiro-Wilk test indicates mild non-normality for X
($W = \resXNormW$, $p = \resXNormP$) while Y is consistent with normality
($W = \resYNormW$, $p = \resYNormP$); given the sample size
($n = \resN$) and the small skewness values, the parametric analysis that
follows is retained and treated as a documented limitation.

This section is important because descriptive statistics provides an
initial overview of the data before we examine relationships between
variables.
